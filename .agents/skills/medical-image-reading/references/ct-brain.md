# CT brain protocol

## Full-study requirement

A diagnostic CT assessment normally requires the ordered series/phase, complete
volume, slice order and thickness, orientation, and appropriate windows. A single
axial screenshot supports only a descriptive single-image observation. Mark the
study incomplete and do not make a global negative claim from one slice.

## Quality gate

Confirm noncontrast/contrast phase when known, series completeness, reconstruction
kernel, slice thickness, motion, coverage, orientation/laterality, and availability
of brain, subdural/blood, and bone windows or equivalent display settings. For a
volume, inspect sequential slices and useful multiplanar views.

## Systematic compartments

1. Extra-axial spaces and calvarium/soft tissues.
2. Ventricles, cisterns, sulci, midline, and mass effect.
3. Cortex and subcortical white matter by hemisphere/lobe.
4. Deep gray nuclei, internal capsules, and corpus callosum when visible.
5. Brainstem, posterior fossa, and cerebellum.
6. Visible vessels, sinuses, orbits, mastoids, and paranasal sinuses as appropriate.

For every retained lesion, bind evidence to series/SOP/frame or an equivalent stable
image ID, window, slice/frame number, laterality, and normalized source coordinates.
Measurements need a stable plane and scale; comparison claims need an identified
prior. Hemorrhage, hydrocephalus, herniation, or significant mass-effect candidates
are urgent review targets, not permission to overstate a partial screenshot.
